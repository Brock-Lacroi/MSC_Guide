      How to read the advice

      This is a synthesis of the transcripts, not a verified live-server rulebook. Much of the material describes closed-beta tests, proposed launch conditions, simulations, or old MapleStory. Class numbers and availability should be read in that context. Third-job material is a planning reference; several creators expected a second-job-only launch.

      Level ranges are starting points. Accuracy, hits per kill, potion use, spawn density, available platforms, and party composition decide when a map becomes worthwhile. A lower-level monster killed quickly can beat a higher-level monster killed slowly.

      Creator names appear once for a shared section or beside a meaningful disagreement. "Recommended by" identifies the source of a recommendation; it does not imply that every creator independently verified every drop or numerical detail. Repeated recommendations are merged, while differing assumptions stay visible.

      The archive supplies 33 transcripts and one map-database URL. Spoken details are retained in usable form; introductions, advertising, repeated commentary, and unspoken on-screen tables are not recreated. Missing recipe ingredients, ambiguous monster names, and uncertain drop claims are left explicit.

      Choosing a main

      Choose the gameplay you can enjoy for a long grind: ranged jump attacks and Haste; melee positioning and shields; elemental map selection and Teleport; or archer range, control, and later movement. Funding and potion comfort matter alongside theoretical damage. A simulator's level-70 damage ranking is not a measured leveling-speed ranking.

      For training, use the second-job comparison first. Use third-job rankings as provisional context rather than a promise of future power. No fourth-job build is established by this archive.

      Tier lists: 2nd job

      The rankings below are creator opinions with different criteria. They are not averaged into a fake consensus. Numerical DPS claims refer to the charts or test assumptions described by the creator, not measured live progression.

      Where the rankings agree

      Ice/Lightning has a distinctive six-target Thunderbolt and excellent Teleport mobility. Its single-target performance and early skill investment are less impressive; dense lightning-weak maps are central to its appeal.

      Assassin offers range, jump attacks, Haste, and strong single-target damage, but no second-job mobbing attack. Bandit has strong Savage Blow damage once developed, but short reach and a two-target Steal limit farming coverage.

      Hunter's Arrow Bomb and spreading Final Attack are attractive for mobbing. Crossbowman favors flat platforms with Iron Arrow. Both lack their own second-job movement skill and can suffer when monsters enter minimum shooting range.

      Warriors all gain Rush. Fighter adds Rage and an axe bleed option; Page offers defense reduction and blunt-weapon defense ignore; Spearman trades some damage for reach and sustain. Whether that makes warriors enjoyable or tedious is a major source of ranking disagreement.

      Mage

      Build guidance is primarily Eistee's, with broader class impressions kept in the tier-list chapters. These are creator recommendations for the described Classic rules; they are not a consensus SP build for every patch.

      First job and early costs

      Eistee describes first advancement at level 10, including mages. Start with one Energy Bolt, develop Magic Claw to 20, then MP Recovery. Magic Claw's two hits and ability to reach through terrain make it the main early attack.

      Delay heavy Magic Guard investment only while the map is safe: redirecting damage into MP can be costly. This is an economy recommendation, not a reason to risk dying. Raise survivability when incoming damage requires it.

      If affordable SP resets actually exist, Eistee discusses moving points out of replaced first-job attacks later. The stated final utility allocation is MP Recovery 15, Max MP 15, Magic Guard 15, and Magic Armor 16, totaling 61. It depends on reset access and on no longer needing the old attacks.

      MSC's general advice to delay attack ranks when they do not reduce hits per kill may be useful during a potion shortage; it is a different priority from Eistee's direct max-Claw route.

      Potion economics

      Fire/Poison: Fire Arrow route

      Take one Teleport early. Eistee prioritizes Fire Arrow, then Meditation, then MP Eater/Teleport, leaving Poison lower and Slow low or unused. Fire Arrow is the preferred practical attack against suitable fire-weak targets.

      A conditional reset route puts nine points into MP Eater through the early 30s, then reallocates toward Fire Arrow around 34. Without known affordable resets, invest directly in the intended attack instead of planning around a refund that may not exist.

      Poison Breath gives a four-target option in the Classic discussions, but its usefulness depends on density and time for poison to work. Coppersan notes few poison-weak targets and a penalty for damage-over-time strategies far above your level. Old poison-to-1-HP channel-hopping should not be imported from Jinjo's historical guide.

      Suggested progression: Evil Eyes roughly 30–34, Jr. Wraiths 34–39, suitable Forgotten Hollow targets, Raffles 47–53, then Sporewood around 59+. The intermediate monster transcribed as "waywood" is unresolved.

      Market and scrolling judgment

      Common materials can have both NPC value and player demand. Compare the next recipe/quest use before selling, but avoid unlimited speculative hoarding. Claims that early assets will inevitably rise in price are predictions, not guaranteed returns.

      Read scroll type, success rate, stat affected, and destruction risk directly. Do not confuse attack power with weapon attack, or a quest's random reward with a chosen reward. A good scrolled lower-level weapon may outlast a clean upgrade, but compare speed and actual damage thresholds.
